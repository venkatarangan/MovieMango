MovieMango

Privacy

Short version: your data stays with you. MovieMango has no server and no accounts. Private · Free · Open.

What stays in your browser

Your lists, ratings, notes and watch links, where your titles stream, taste portrait, settings and API keys are stored in your browser (IndexedDB), and in your own Google Drive if you connect it (you can keep keys out of Drive in Settings). Backup files you download never include your keys.

Google Drive

If you sign in with Google, MovieMango saves one file to a hidden app folder in your own Google Drive, using the drive.appdata permission. It also reads your email address (the openid and email permissions) to show which account is connected. MovieMango can’t see, open or change any of your other files. The data goes directly from your browser to Google. Disconnect in Settings. To delete the Drive copy, remove MovieMango under Google Drive → Settings → Manage apps, or revoke access from your Google Account’s security page.

MovieMango’s use of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Who else MovieMango talks to

  • TMDB: searches, title details, where titles stream, episode lists and the titles you import, using your own key. Titles you looked at recently are kept in your browser, so MovieMango asks TMDB less often.
  • Wikipedia and Wikidata: background summaries and, if you turn it on, today’s headlines.
  • WordPress.com: Mangoidiots reviews, if turned on.
  • Google Gemini API: only if you add your own key and use the cloud engine. On Google’s free tier, prompts may be used to improve Google’s products.
  • Hugging Face: a one-time model download if you choose the Qwen engine.
  • Streaming services: only when you tap Play.

Analytics

The public site at watch.mangoidiots.com uses Google Analytics to count how MovieMango is used, so the author can see what is worth improving. It is on by default, and you can turn it off any time in Settings → Privacy (“Share anonymous usage stats”). Once it’s off, nothing more is sent, and if it’s off when you open the app, the Google Analytics script isn’t even loaded. No personal information is collected.

What is counted

  • Page views: only the kind of page, with any ids removed (for example “/title/movie”, never which title) and no search text in the address.
  • Feature use: the name of a feature plus a few small, non-identifying details. For example: search was used (never what you searched for); which streaming service’s Play button was tapped; which rating button was used (never for which title); a title was added to or removed from Watchlist, Watched or a custom list (never the list’s name or its titles); what kind of share and how; an import started or finished, with counts; a Tonight run, with the AI engine, the minutes chosen, whether discovery was on and whether you typed something (never what you typed); onboarding finished, with counts; Google Drive connected or failed; keys were saved (the fact only, never the key); notes were saved (which field, never the content); an episode was marked; a custom title was added, edited or deleted.

What is never sent: titles, list names or contents, search words, moods or anything you type, your notes or links, ratings tied to titles, API keys, your Google account email, or anything from your Drive.

Advertising is off. Ads storage, ad personalisation, ad user data and Google signals are all switched off, and the data is not used for ads.

Cookies and your IP address. Google Analytics sets a first-party cookie holding a random ID, which tells one visit from another. It isn’t linked to your name or email. As with any website, Google sees your IP address briefly while serving the request, and uses it to estimate your country and city; Google Analytics doesn’t store it.

What the author sees. Only aggregate reports: counts of pages and features, countries and device types. Never anything that identifies a person. There is no MovieMango server. An ad blocker may block Google Analytics; that’s fine, MovieMango works exactly the same.
